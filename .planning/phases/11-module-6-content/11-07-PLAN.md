Add ~15 exam questions covering all Module 6 lesson topics. Follow existing pattern: 4 options, correctIndex, explanation, difficulty, moduleId: 6.

**Distribution:** 40% hard (6), 40% medium (6), 20% easy (3) = 15 questions

**Easy (3):**
- q6-1: "What are the two goals of the Fed's Dual Mandate?" (maximum employment + stable prices)
- q6-2: "How many members serve on the FOMC?" (12: 7 governors + NY Fed president + 4 rotating)
- q6-3: "What is the money multiplier formula?" (1 / reserve ratio)

**Medium (6):**
- q6-4: "Why was the Swedish Riksbank founded in 1668?" (to control money supply after private bank over-issued notes and collapsed)
- q6-5: "What happens to the money supply when the Fed conducts an open market purchase?" (reserves increase → banks lend more → money supply expands)
- q6-6: "What is the primary purpose of the discount window?" (provide short-term cash to banks against collateral; acts as rate ceiling)
- q6-7: "What does a 'dot' in the Dot Plot represent?" (an individual FOMC participant's expectation for the future fed funds rate)
- q6-8: "How did QE1 affect mortgage rates?" (30-year fixed fell from ~6.5% to ~4.85% as MBS purchases drove down MBS yields)
- q6-9: "What is the difference between M1 and M2?" (M2 adds savings deposits, small time deposits, and retail MMMF shares to M1)

**Hard (6):**
- q6-10: "Why did the relationship between money supply and GDP break down after 2008?" (QE flooded system with reserves; new payment technologies made measurement harder; M3 tracking inflation/GDP became unreliable)
- q6-11: "How does IORB create a floor for the federal funds rate?" (banks won't lend below IORB because they can earn that rate risk-free at the Fed; arbitrage ensures convergence)
- q6-12: "Why is ON RRP needed in addition to IORB?" (non-banks like money market funds can't access IORB; without ON RRP they might lend below IORB, pulling short-term rates down)
- q6-13: "What is the key difference between Operation Twist and QE?" (Twist sells short-term and buys long-term — no net balance sheet expansion; QE creates new reserves to buy securities — balance sheet expands)
- q6-14: "In the abundant reserves regime, why are traditional OMOs less effective?" (with massive reserves, small changes don't shift the supply-demand equilibrium enough to move the fed funds rate; need administered rates instead)
- q6-15: "How does the Fed's balance sheet expansion mechanically work — does it print paper money?" (No — the Fed credits banks' reserve accounts digitally; reserves grow on the liability side while purchased securities grow on the asset side)

Each question needs:

- question: clear English
- options: 4 plausible choices (one correct)
- correctIndex: 0-3
- explanation: 1-2 sentence explanation of why the answer is correct
- difficulty: "easy" | "medium" | "hard"
- moduleId: 6
- conceptId: reference to a module 6 concept ID
