# Phase 11: Module 6 Content - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-02-21
**Status:** Ready for planning

<vision>
## How This Should Work

Module 6 organizes monetary policy concepts into tools-centric clusters rather than following lecture order. Three self-contained dashboards group related ideas so students see connections:

1. **Structure & Mandate** — Why central banks exist, how the Fed is organized, its five functions, and the Dual Mandate that frames everything.

2. **Traditional Toolkit** — How the Fed controls rates in "normal times": money supply aggregates, money multiplier, Fed balance sheet mechanics, open market operations (reserve supply/demand), and the discount window (rate corridor upper bound).

3. **Crisis Toolkit** — What happens when traditional tools aren't enough: forward guidance (SEP, Dot Plot), large-scale asset purchases (QE1-3, Operation Twist), and the abundant reserves framework (IORB floor for banks, ON RRP floor for non-banks).

Each cluster feels like a Fed operations dashboard — clean panels with dials, charts, and controls. Professional but not boring. Students should feel like they're sitting at the Fed's trading desk, turning knobs and watching outcomes change.

</vision>

<essential>
## What Must Be Nailed

- **Rate mechanics clarity** — Students must *see* how OMOs shift reserve supply, how the discount rate caps the corridor, and how IORB/ON RRP create a floor. The rate-determination story is the core.
- **Before vs after 2008 contrast** — The big aha-moment is traditional vs unconventional. Pre-2008 world feels simple and small, then everything changes — balance sheet explosion, new tools, floor system.
- **Every concept interactive** — No concept should be just text. Money multiplier needs sliders, balance sheet needs interactive components, QE needs yield charts. Full interactivity across every topic.

</essential>

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- **Dashboard-style panels** — Each cluster feels like a Fed operations dashboard with clean panels, dials, charts, and controls. Professional but engaging. Students feel like they're at the Fed's trading desk.
- **Tools-centric grouping** — Concepts clustered by what the Fed actually does, not by lecture order. Three groups: structure & mandate, traditional toolkit, crisis toolkit.
- **Every visualization interactive** — Sliders, toggles, calculators for every major concept. Money multiplier with reserve ratio slider, balance sheet with expandable components, supply/demand curves that shift with OMO actions, yield charts that respond to QE programs.

</specifics>

<notes>
## Additional Context

All three essentials (rate mechanics, pre/post-2008 contrast, full interactivity) are equally important — none takes priority over the others.

The module covers 10 lessons across 3 lesson groups:
- Lesson 6-0 (Overview) + Lesson 6-1 (Fed history, structure, functions)
- Lesson 6-2 (Traditional: money supply, balance sheet, OMOs, discount window)
- Lesson 6-3 (Non-traditional: forward guidance, LSAP/QE, abundant reserves/IORB/ON RRP)

Source document covers extensive detail including: Swedish Riksbank case study, Panic of 1907, Federal Reserve Act 1913, Board of Governors/12 Reserve Banks/FOMC composition, five Fed functions, Monetary Base/M1/M2/M3, money multiplier deposit cycle, Quantity Theory of Money, Fed balance sheet pre-2008 vs 2021, Government Securities/MBS/Crisis Facilities, SOMA/Primary Dealers implementation chain, discount loan mechanics with collateral haircuts, SEP/Dot Plot expectations channel, QE1-3/Operation Twist with yield evidence, IORB arbitrage logic, ON RRP for non-banks.

</notes>
